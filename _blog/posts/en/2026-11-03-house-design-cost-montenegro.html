What you are buying: three stages

If you come from a country where "the plans" are one package, this is the first thing to adjust to. Here, the design moves through stages, and each one has a different job:

  1. Plot check and layout. We confirm what the plan allows (use, floors, coverage and density ratios, building line), then fit your brief to it.
  2. Concept design (idejno rješenje). Site plan, floor plans, sections, elevations and 3D. This is what the chief city architect (glavni gradski arhitekta) approves. You cannot build from it.
  3. Main design (glavni projekat). The full set the builder works from: architecture, structure with calculations, plumbing, electrical and heating, plus a bill of quantities.

You can order all of it or only the stage you need. So a surprisingly low quote is often not cheaper at all. It may simply stop at the concept design.

What pushes the price up

  • A sloping plot. This is the big one on the coast. A slope brings retaining walls, drainage and more involved foundations.
  • The shape of the house. Long spans, cantilevers, usable flat roofs and pools all take more structural work than a simple plan.
  • Strict plan rules. Tight ratios or rules on roofs and materials mean more options tried before one works.
  • A rental unit. It affects the layout and may affect how the building is treated, so tell us early.
  • Building services. Underfloor heating, heat pumps, solar panels or a septic tank where there is no public sewer all add design work.
  • Urgency. The calculator on our prices page marks urgent work as +30%.

That is why a price per square metre quoted over the phone means little until someone has seen the plot.

Should you pay for design before you buy the plot?

Not for a full design. But do have the plot checked before you commit to it, because a plan check is the step that protects the rest of your budget. Coverage ratios, floor limits and the building line decide whether the house you have in mind fits at all. A plot with a lovely view and a tight building line can leave little room for it. A layout drawn before that check may have to be redrawn.

Once you own the plot, order the survey straight away. Then the concept design can be priced and drawn on real terrain, not on assumptions.

How to compare two quotes

  1. Do both cover the same stages and the same disciplines: architecture, structure, plumbing and drainage, electrical, heating and ventilation?
  2. Is the review included, coordinated or left to you?
  3. Who pays for the geotechnical report, and who orders it?
  4. Are corrections after the chief city architect's comments included? Ours are, at no extra cost.
  5. Do you get a bill of quantities, so builders can price the same thing?
  6. Is the price fixed, or an estimate that can grow? Is VAT stated?
  7. Who signs? Documents have to be signed by licensed professionals. In our case, signature and review go through licensed partners.
  8. Will one person answer you in English from start to finish, or will you chase each discipline yourself?

Common mistakes

  1. Budgeting from the building cost per m² and leaving out surveys, reports and municipal charges.
  2. Comparing a concept-only quote with a complete set of documents.
  3. Buying the plot first and asking what the plan allows afterwards.
  4. Not telling the architect the build budget, then redesigning once builders have priced it.
  5. Expecting plans bought abroad to save most of the fee. They still have to be adapted to the UTU and approved.

What to send for an exact price

  • The plot number and municipality, or the UTU if you already have it.
  • The number of floors and rough floor area you have in mind.
  • Who will live there, plus any extras: rental unit, garage, pool.
  • A sketch, a photo or a picture of a house you like.
  • Your rough build budget.
  • The stage you need: concept design, main design or everything.
