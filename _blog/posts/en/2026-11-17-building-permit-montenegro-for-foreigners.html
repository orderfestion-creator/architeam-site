Short answer: a building permit in Montenegro for foreigners follows the same route as for anyone else who owns a plot. On our building permit page the rule is stated by ownership, not nationality: the owner of the plot, an individual or a company, can build once the required documents are in place. The questions that decide your project are different ones: who is registered as the owner, what the plan allows on that plot, and what type of building you want. This article walks through the order of steps and the points where foreign owners most often lose time.

We are designers, not lawyers. Buying land, whether you can hold a particular plot, taxes and contracts are for a licensed Montenegrin lawyer or notary. Procedures also differ by municipality and building type, so we check your case separately before giving advice.

Three questions that come before any drawing

  1. Who owns the plot, and in what form? The owner (a person or a company) is who applies and signs. The ownership extract from the property register is the starting document, and a lawyer should confirm it.
  2. What does the plan allow? Use, number of floors, coverage and density ratios, building line. This is the cheapest check you will make, and it decides whether your idea fits at all.
  3. What kind of building is it? A family house, a villa, a small apartment building and a hall are treated differently, and this decides which filing you make at the end. We confirm that for your case rather than guess.

The sequence, step by step

This is the order set out on our permit page. We do not repeat time ranges here, because different pages of ours and different municipalities give different numbers. The schedule is fixed in the offer.

StepWhat it isWho is responsible
1. Plot and plan checkUse, floors, ratios, building lineDesigner
2. Urban-technical conditions (UTU)The official document with building parameters; the design is based on itMunicipality / authority
3. Topographic surveyExact terrain, boundaries and existing structuresLicensed surveyor
4. Concept design (idejno rješenje)Plans, elevations, sections and 3D to the UTUArchitect
5. Chief city architect approvalCheck against the plan and architectural guidelinesGlavni gradski arhitekta
6. Main design (glavni projekat)Architecture, structure, building services: the set you build fromLicensed designers
7. Review and filingAn authorised firm reviews the main design; then a notice or a permit application is filed, depending on building typeReview firm, client

Two things in this sequence surprise people who come from countries with a single "planning permission" step. First, the concept design cannot be built from. It exists to be approved. Second, the review of the main design is a real step, not a stamp. Skipping ahead is how months get lost.

Where it helps to be present, and where it does not

Foreign owners often ask whether they must live in Montenegro for this. Much of the practical work happens on site and is done by the designer and licensed partners: the survey, the contact with the municipality, the filing. The design itself can be discussed remotely, and a sketch, a photo or a picture from the internet is enough to start a conversation.

What is less obvious is which steps will need your signature or a power of attorney. That depends on the municipality and the type of building. Ask this at the start of the project. If it comes up only before filing, it is the kind of surprise that costs weeks.

Common mistakes of foreign owners

  1. Ordering a design before the plot is checked. A house you saw elsewhere may not fit the coverage ratio, the floor limit or the building line of your plot. A layout drawn first may have to be redrawn.
  2. Assuming plans from abroad solve most of the work. They can be a starting point for the layout. They still have to be fitted to the UTU, the structure and building services have to be worked out for the real site, and the documents have to be issued by designers licensed in Montenegro.
  3. Treating the plan check as a legal check. We can tell you what the plan allows. We cannot tell you whether a purchase is safe. Use a lawyer for that, in parallel.
  4. Comparing quotes by price only. One quote may stop at the concept design, another may go through review. Our guide to house design cost in Montenegro lists the questions to ask.
  5. Building before the documents are complete. A house that is already standing without a permit is a different, slower matter: legalisation, which is not always possible.

What is worth doing before you buy a plot

Check the plan before you sign anything, because it tells you whether the plot can hold the house you want. Then ask for the UTU conditions if they are available for the plot, and think about the terrain. A sloping plot, common on the coast, brings retaining walls and more involved foundations, which changes what the project will involve. None of this replaces the legal checks of the purchase, and none of it is a reason to delay them.

What to send us

  • The cadastral parcel number and municipality, or the UTU if you already have it, and the ownership extract.
  • Photos of the plot and, if you have one, the topographic survey.
  • How many people will live there, the floors and rough floor area you have in mind, and extras such as a garage, pool or rental unit.
  • A sketch or pictures of houses you like, and your rough build budget.
  • Whether you will be on site or would act through someone else.
